How many days have I been alive?

Subtract your birth date from today's date and count the days in between. Someone born on 15 March 1990 had lived 13,353 days by 5 October 2026, about 36.6 years. Count the whole years, add one extra day for every leap day you passed, then add the days since your last birthday.

Why this happens

The number is simple, but it is easy to get wrong by a few days. A year is not always 365 days: the Gregorian calendar adds a 29 February in most years divisible by four, and skips it in century years unless they divide by 400. That is why 1900 was not a leap year while 2000 was. Those extra days are the whole reason a rough guess of age times 365 is always a little short.

There is also a quiet choice hidden in the question: do you count the day you were born, and do you count today? Most calculators treat the difference between two dates as the number of midnights crossed, so the birth day itself is day zero. Pick one convention and keep it, and your answer will match theirs.

Seeing the number can feel strange. Thirteen thousand days is a big figure and a small one at the same time, and either reaction is normal. It is simply a count of the days behind you.

How to fix it without an app

  1. Write down both dates and choose a counting rule

    Write your birth date and today's date in full, day, month and year. Decide whether the day you were born counts as day zero or day one. The usual convention, and the one date calculators use, is that the birth day is day zero and each midnight after it adds one, so a person born yesterday has lived exactly 1 day. Writing the rule at the top of your page matters more than it sounds, because a one day difference is the most common reason two answers disagree. The worked example below uses 15 March 1990 as the birth date and 5 October 2026 as today, which are only sample dates.

    Time: 2 minutes · You need: Pen and paper, or a notes app

  2. Count the whole years and convert them to days

    Count how many full birthdays you have had. For the example, 15 March 1990 to 15 March 2026 is exactly 36 years. Multiply by 365: 36 × 365 = 13,140 days. This is the bulk of the answer and it is also the part that is always slightly short, which the next step repairs. If you were born on 29 February, treat 28 February or 1 March as your birthday consistently in this step and note which one you chose so you do not shift the total by a day.

    Time: 3 minutes · You need: A calculator or the phone calculator

  3. Add one day for every leap day you lived through

    List the leap years between your birth year and this year, using the rule: divisible by 4, except century years that are not divisible by 400. In the example the 29 Februaries that fall between 15 March 1990 and 15 March 2026 are in 1992, 1996, 2000, 2004, 2008, 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024. That is 9 extra days. The year 1990 itself is not counted, because it is not a leap year. If you were born before 29 February in a leap year, that same year's leap day counts for you. Add the 9: 13,140 + 9 = 13,149 days.

    Time: 3 minutes · You need: A list of years, the leap year rule

  4. Add the days since your last birthday

    Now count from your most recent birthday to today. For the example, 15 March 2026 to 5 October 2026 means: the rest of March after the 15th is 16 days, then April 30, May 31, June 30, July 31, August 31 and September 30, then 5 days in October. 16 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 + 30 + 5 = 204. Add that to the previous total: 13,149 + 204 = 13,353 days. If this stretch crosses a 29 February, add one more day for it.

    Time: 4 minutes · You need: A month length table

  5. Check the result with a spreadsheet and turn it into weeks and years

    Open any spreadsheet, type your birth date in cell A1 and today's date in A2, and in A3 enter the formula =A2-A1. Spreadsheets store dates as day numbers, so the cell returns the same total, and it already handles every leap year for you. If it agrees with your hand count, your arithmetic is right. If it differs by exactly one, look back at the counting rule from the first step. For other units, divide by 7 for weeks (13,353 ÷ 7 is about 1,907.6) and by 365.25 for years (about 36.6). Multiply by 24 for hours, if you want a bigger number.

    Time: 3 minutes · You need: Any spreadsheet app

Where this approach stops working

  • It is a snapshot. The number is correct for the moment you counted and wrong by tomorrow, so you would need to redo the sum or reopen the sheet every time you want an up-to-date figure.
  • Hand counting is where mistakes live. Skipping a leap day, using the wrong month length, or switching the counting rule midway each shifts the answer by a day or more, and nothing warns you.
  • Being born on 29 February, or in a different time zone from where you are now, makes the edge cases fuzzier. A spreadsheet will give a precise answer, but you still have to decide how to read it.
  • The result only looks backward. A count of days lived says nothing about the days ahead, and the figures that do exist are population averages, not a forecast for any one person.
  • A bare number has no feeling attached. Thirteen thousand days is hard to picture, so many people read it once and forget it.

Questions people also ask

Does the day I was born count?

By the usual date calculator convention it does not: the birth day is day zero, and every midnight after it adds one. Someone born yesterday has lived 1 day. If you prefer to count the birth day as day one, add one to the result and keep the same rule every time.

How many days is 30 years?

About 10,957 days, because a year averages 365.25 days once leap years are included. The exact count depends on how many 29 Februaries fall in the span, which is usually 7 or 8 over 30 years.

How long does an average person live?

Population averages are published by official statistics bodies. The WHO reports global life expectancy at birth of 73.3 years in 2023. The CDC's National Center for Health Statistics reports 79.0 years for the United States in 2024, with 76.5 for males and 81.4 for females. These are averages across whole populations, not a prediction for any one person, and they say nothing about how long you in particular will live.

I feel low when I see this kind of number. What should I do?

That reaction is common, and it is fine to close the page. If thoughts about ending your life ever come up, you do not have to carry them alone: in the US you can call or text 988 at any hour, and findahelpline.com lists verified helplines by country.
