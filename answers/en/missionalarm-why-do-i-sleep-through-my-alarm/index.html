Why do I sleep through my alarm?

Most often it is too little sleep or a wake time that keeps shifting, and sometimes sleep inertia, the grogginess that lasts 15 to 60 minutes after waking. Aim for seven or more hours, keep one wake time every day, and put the alarm across the room. If you stay very sleepy in the daytime, see a clinician.

Why this happens

Sleeping through an alarm is rarely a willpower problem. The CDC lists seven or more hours a night as the recommendation for adults aged 18 to 60, and NHLBI describes sleep deficiency as broader than short sleep: sleeping at the wrong times, poor sleep quality, or an untreated sleep disorder count too. If your body is short on sleep, you may simply not surface when the alarm goes off.

The second piece is how you feel after you do wake. The Sleep Foundation describes sleep inertia as the grogginess, disorientation and drowsiness that immediately follow waking, generally lasting 15 to 60 minutes. In that state the brain is working poorly, so turning off the alarm and drifting back under can happen with almost no memory of it.

Both causes are common and ordinary. The fixes below target them one at a time, and they cost nothing but a few days of consistency.

How to fix it without an app

  1. Work out how many hours you actually sleep

    For one week, write down when you get into bed and when you get up. Compare it with the CDC figure of seven or more hours for adults. If you are regularly under it, no alarm trick will fully fix the problem, because the cause is the shortfall. Move bedtime earlier in 15-minute steps rather than all at once.

    Time: 1 week of notes, 1 minute per day · You need: A notes app or paper

  2. Fix one wake time, weekends included

    The Sleep Foundation advises waking at the same time on weekdays and weekends, because a fluctuating schedule keeps you from getting into a rhythm of consistent sleep. The CDC gives the same advice: go to bed and get up at the same time every day. A steady wake time makes the alarm arrive when your body is already close to surfacing.

    Time: Ongoing, noticeable in 1 to 2 weeks · You need: Nothing

  3. Put the alarm across the room

    Cleveland Clinic suggests setting the alarm for when you actually need to wake up and placing it across the room. Having to stand up and cross the floor gets you out of bed before you can switch it off. Put the phone on a shelf or dresser that is out of arm's reach, plugged in.

    Time: 1 minute tonight · You need: A charger and a spot across the room

  4. Get light on your eyes straight away

    The Sleep Foundation notes that light, especially sunlight, is one of the key drivers of the circadian rhythm. Open the curtains or step outside as soon as you are up, and do not go back to a dark room. The same page mentions sunrise-style alarms that wake you with gradually increasing light.

    Time: 5 to 10 minutes each morning · You need: A window or a doorstep

  5. Protect the night before

    The CDC recommends a quiet, relaxing, cool bedroom, turning off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime, and avoiding caffeine in the afternoon or evening. These do not wake you up, but they change how good the sleep is that the alarm has to cut through.

    Time: 30 minutes before bed · You need: A bedtime routine

Where this approach stops working

  • These steps take days to weeks to show up. They help most when the cause is a sleep shortfall or an irregular schedule, and they do nothing on the one morning you are already running on four hours.
  • Across-the-room placement fails if you walk over, turn the alarm off and walk straight back to bed. Standing up is only the first half of waking up.
  • Sleep inertia can last up to a few hours in some cases, according to the Sleep Foundation, so feeling foggy after getting up is not proof that the plan is failing.
  • Shift work, a newborn, or an irregular job can make a fixed wake time impossible, and then the advice above only partly applies.
  • If you sleep what should be enough hours and still cannot wake, or you are very sleepy during the day, habits are not the whole answer. NHLBI says to tell a healthcare provider if you often feel sleepy during the day or do not wake up feeling refreshed and alert.

Questions people also ask

Why can I sleep through a loud alarm?

There is no single answer. The two common explanations are sleep deficiency, where you are not getting enough or good enough sleep, and sleep inertia right after waking. NHLBI notes that sleep deficiency also covers poor sleep quality and untreated sleep disorders.

How long does morning grogginess last?

The Sleep Foundation says sleep inertia generally lasts 15 to 60 minutes, but may last up to a few hours after waking.

How many hours of sleep do I need?

The CDC recommends seven or more hours a night for adults aged 18 to 60. Needs vary between people, so use that as a floor to check against.

When should I see a doctor about oversleeping?

If you often feel sleepy during the day or do not wake up feeling refreshed and alert, NHLBI advises telling your healthcare provider. A clinician can look at possible causes. An alarm app does not diagnose or treat anything.

Will a mission alarm fix the problem?

It helps with one part: getting you properly awake once the alarm sounds. It does not add hours to your night, so the sleep-schedule steps above still matter.
